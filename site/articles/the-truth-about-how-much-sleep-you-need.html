    • The Truth About How Much Sleep You Need

    If you've ever searched online for the "perfect" amount of sleep, you've probably come away more confused than when you started.

    You've heard "8 hours." Maybe you've also heard someone at a networking event

    brag about thriving on 5. Maybe that someone was you.

    Here's the honest answer: most adults need between 7 and 9 hours. Not as a

    suggestion, but as a biological requirement for full cognitive function, emotional

    regulation, immune health and metabolic balance.

    The right amount of sleep is the amount that lets you wake without an alarm, feel

    alert within 20 minutes of rising, and sustain focus and emotional steadiness through

    the day.

     

    The nappuccino and the holy power nap

    Now for one of the most delightful and evidence-backed tools in the whole sleep

    world. It has a name that sounds like something from a movie: the nappuccino.

    Also known as the coffee nap. And it is exactly what it sounds like.

    Here's the mechanism: You drink a coffee or espresso. Then you immediately lie

    down and nap for 20 minutes. When you wake up, the caffeine, which takes about

    20–25 minutes to be absorbed into your bloodstream is just starting to kick in.

    The result? You wake from the nap feeling more alert and refreshed than either

    coffee or a nap alone would produce.

    But, and this matters enormously, the nap window is critical. Power naps should be:

    ·  20 minutes or less -to avoid entering deep sleep

    ·  before 3pm if possible - later naps begin to interfere with night time sleep

    schedule

    The 20-minute sweet spot keeps you in light, restorative, not disorienting sleep. If

    you can't nap for different various reasons: open office, back-to-back calls, general

    social judgment, even lying down with your eyes closed for 10 minutes offers partial

    cognitive restoration.

    The key takeaway is simple: getting enough sleep isn't about hitting a magic number of hours. It's about giving your brain and body enough time to recover and perform at their best.

     

    The body doing nothing is still doing something.
